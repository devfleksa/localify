## Localization rules (shared by the scan and the planner)

**We can edit:**
- Overlay text that is part of the ad's message: headlines, captions and meme text burned into the image, prices and
  currencies, calls to action, dates and date formats. (Rewrite it in the market's copy language; same place, similar
  length.)
- Props, food and drinks that are *not* the brand's product: a cup of coffee on a fashion shoot, a grill at a picnic,
  a flag, a holiday decoration. They can be swapped for the local equivalent.
- The place behind the subject (street, city, billboard location, station, landscape) when it is clearly a place:
  it can move to a recognisable place in the market.

**Copy language:** each market has one copy language (its market file's "Copy language"). Informational and
explanatory overlay copy (captions, meme text, offers, prices, CTAs, dates) goes into that language, even when the
original is English and English is understood there (e.g. India → Hindi). Only the brand's tagline or slogan (a short
signature line such as "Just Do It") stays exactly as the brand wrote it.

**We never edit (they are not findings to change):**
- The brand's own product, packaging, logo, brand name and the ad artwork itself.
  (A coffee brand's drink, a fashion brand's clothes, a car brand's car are the product.)
- Product and menu item names, including drink names (e.g. "PSL", "Unicorn Frappuccino", a dish on the menu). They are
  the brand's naming, never a finding, even when they are in English.
- People, faces, bodies, hair, makeup, clothing and poses. If something about them could be sensitive in a market,
  it is a **review** item for a human, never an edit.
- Text that is only a logo, a hashtag, a handle, a product name, or tiny unreadable print.

**How much it matters (strength):**
- `must`: the post would be a cultural mistake or offence in that market as it is (anything listed under the market's
  **Protect** rules), or it shows wrong information for the market (a price in another currency, a date or offer that
  only applies elsewhere).
- `strong`: a clear, worthwhile local touch: readable overlay copy that is not in the market's copy language, or a
  scene or prop that would clearly feel foreign and has an obvious local equivalent. Three cases are always `strong`,
  for every market:
  - **the place in any outdoor scene**: street, park, city view, café terrace, waterfront, and nature (river, lake,
    forest, mountain, beach, desert, countryside road), even when it is generic or out of focus: moving it to a famous
    place in the market is the main local touch;
  - the place around an ad shown in an outdoor placement (billboard, bus shelter, metro or station panel, wall poster,
    shop window), even a generic skyline or street;
  - cooking, grill or picnic gear in an outdoor scene (camping stove, barbecue, grill): every market has a clear local
    equivalent (mangal, churrasco, yakiniku grill, tandoor…).
- **The background is never a finding** when it is a studio or plain backdrop, when it is mostly hidden (a close-up of
  a product, face or hand where little of the place shows), indoors (home, office, shop interior), the brand's own
  store, or a landmark that is the point of the post.
- `weak`: technically possible but not worth an edit (a neutral prop, small background details).
  When in doubt, it is `weak`.

A post needs localizing for a market only if it has at least one editable `must` or `strong` change for that market.
Most brand posts (studio product shots, model shots, plain lifestyle images) need nothing.
