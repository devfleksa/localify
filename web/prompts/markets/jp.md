# Japan (JP)
Copy language: Japanese · Script: kanji, hiragana and katakana, horizontal in ads (vertical also used) · Currency: ¥1,980 or 1,980円 (tax included) · Date: 2026年10月14日 or 10/14 · Numbers: 1,980

## Protect
- The number 4 (shi, sounds like "death") as a gift count or set size, e.g. "4-piece gift set" or four items in a gift box.
- Chopsticks standing upright in a bowl of rice, or food passed from chopsticks to chopsticks (funeral customs).
- Shoes worn on tatami, in a home's living area or on a bed.
- White chrysanthemums or a black-and-white flower arrangement in a festive scene (funeral flowers).
- A price in another currency, or a yen price marked as before tax (税抜): Japan requires tax-included prices.
- The Rising Sun flag (sun with rays) as decoration; it is sensitive across Asia.
- Tattoos shown prominently next to onsen, gym or beach themes (review if on a person).

## Language & formats
- **All localized copy in Japanese**, polite and warm (です/ます style). Short phrases; a mix of kanji, kana and some English words written in katakana is normal in ads.
- Japanese text is much shorter than English in characters: keep the same visual weight, use fewer, larger characters.
- Price: "¥1,980" or "1,980円（税込）". Prices often end in 80 or 90 (1,980円, 2,990円).
- Date: 2026年10月14日, "10/14（火）" with the weekday in brackets, or "10月14日まで". Time: 24-hour or 午後3時. Units: metric.
- CTAs: "今すぐチェック" (check now), "詳しくはこちら" (learn more), "ご購入はこちら" (buy here), "期間限定" (limited time), "新発売" (new release), "送料無料" (free shipping).
- Rendering: tested 2026-10-15, the edit model writes two caption-size lines in this script correctly; keep new text about as long as the original, in a clean bold sans-serif. Use a gothic (sans-serif) font.
- Seasonal themes matter: sakura (spring, March–April), summer festivals and fireworks (July–August), autumn leaves (momiji, November), New Year (Oshōgatsu).

## Objects & food
Swap for the Japanese equivalent that plays the same role (same size, position and use). Describe it visually:
- **Takeaway coffee, mug → matcha or canned coffee**: bright green matcha latte in a clear cup, or a ceramic chawan bowl with frothy matcha and a bamboo whisk; a slim can of coffee from a vending machine.
- **Tea glass, teapot → Japanese tea set**: cast-iron or clay kyūsu teapot with small handleless cups of green tea.
- **Sandwich, wrap, burger → onigiri or bento**: triangle rice balls wrapped in nori; a compartmented bento box with rice, grilled salmon, tamagoyaki and pickles.
- **Pastry, donut, cake → wagashi or Japanese strawberry shortcake**: pastel mochi and dango on a skewer; white sponge cake with whipped cream and strawberries.
- **Pizza, kebab, street food → ramen, takoyaki or yakitori**: bowl of ramen with chashu, egg and nori; takoyaki balls with sauce and bonito flakes in a boat tray; yakitori skewers.
- **Barbecue, grill → yakiniku table grill or yakitori grill**: small charcoal shichirin grill with thin slices of beef and vegetables.
- **Breakfast → Japanese breakfast**: tray with rice, miso soup, grilled fish, natto and pickles.
- **Soft drink → ramune or green tea bottle**: ramune glass bottle with a marble in the neck; PET bottle of green tea.
- **Decor, lucky charm → maneki-neko (beckoning cat), daruma doll, paper lanterns, noren curtain**, furin wind chime in summer.
- **Picnic → hanami picnic**: blue tarp under cherry blossoms, bento boxes, paper cups.

## Places
### Billboard / skyline / big outdoor ad
- **Shibuya Crossing, Tokyo**: the scramble crossing seen from above, crowds crossing in every direction, huge video screens on the Q-Front and station buildings.
- **Shinjuku (east exit, Kabukichō)**: dense tall buildings covered in signs and screens, the 3D cat billboard area at Cross Shinjuku Vision.
- **Dōtonbori, Osaka**: canal lined with giant signs, the Glico running man sign, Ebisubashi bridge, mechanical crab sign.
- **Ginza Chūō-dōri**: wide avenue of flagship fashion stores, the Wakō building clock tower at the Ginza 4-chōme crossing.
- **Tokyo Tower or Tokyo Skytree skyline**: red-and-white lattice tower, or the tall white Skytree over low-rise Asakusa.

### Shopping street / shop window / wall poster
- **Omotesandō, Tokyo**: zelkova-tree-lined avenue, architect-designed flagship stores (glass and concrete), stylish shoppers. Best match for fashion brands.
- **Harajuku Takeshita-dōri**: narrow pedestrian street packed with colourful youth fashion shops and crêpe stands.
- **Shimokitazawa**: small vintage and second-hand shops, low buildings, tangled overhead wires.
- **Kyoto Gion (Hanamikōji)**: traditional wooden machiya houses with lattice fronts, paper lanterns, stone paving. Respectful scenes only.
- **Nakamise-dōri, Asakusa**: red lacquered shop stalls leading to Sensō-ji's gate with the giant red lantern (temple in the distance only).

### Bus stop / street-level transit
- **Toei bus stop in central Tokyo**: simple pole sign with timetable or small shelter, green-and-white Toei bus, clean sidewalk, vending machines nearby.
- **Taxi rank in Ginza**: black or dark-green "JapanTaxi" cabs with lit roof signs, neat queues.
- **Pedestrian crossing in a residential street**: zebra crossing, utility poles with dense overhead wires, convenience store (konbini) on the corner.

### Metro / station
Japanese stations are spotless, crowded and practical, with dense colourful signage. Describe them as they are: light grey
tiled floors with yellow tactile paving, platform screen doors, line colour stripes, numbered station codes (e.g. "G09"),
bilingual signs (Japanese and English), backlit ad frames, orderly queues. Not futuristic.
- **Shinjuku Station**: the world's busiest station, wide corridors, JR line signs in green (Yamanote) and orange (Chūō), crowds.
- **Tokyo Station Marunouchi side**: red-brick domed station building (exterior), or busy underground passages.
- **Tokyo Metro Ginza Line platform**: orange line colour, compact platform, line code "G".
- **Shinkansen platform**: white bullet train with a long nose, platform with painted queue markings, ekiben shop.

### Roadside / landscape / coast
- **Mount Fuji from Lake Kawaguchi**: snow-capped symmetric volcano reflected in the lake, cherry trees or autumn leaves on the shore.
- **Chūreitō Pagoda (Arakurayama)**: five-story red pagoda in front of Mount Fuji (classic postcard view).
- **Arashiyama bamboo grove, Kyoto**: path through tall green bamboo.
- **Hakone**: green mountains, Lake Ashi with the red torii in the water (distant), winding mountain road.
- **Hokkaido (Furano / Biei)**: rolling hills of lavender and flower fields in summer, deep snow fields in winter.
- **Okinawa coast**: white sand, turquoise water, red-tiled roofs with shisa lion statues.
- **Rural mountain road**: narrow two-lane road through cedar forest, guardrails, a small vending machine by the road.

### Night
Shibuya and Shinjuku neon at night, Dōtonbori signs reflected in the canal, a small izakaya alley with red lanterns (Omoide Yokochō), Tokyo Tower lit orange.

## Keep it real
Clean streets, narrow roads, small boxy kei cars and white Toyota taxis, vending machines everywhere, utility poles with
dense overhead wires, konbini (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart) on corners, cyclists on sidewalks, people in neat office
wear. Signs dense and bilingual. Keep background signs few, short or out of focus; garbled Japanese text looks fake.

## Never
- Never put shrines or temples as the focus behind a commercial ad (distant only).
- Never show chopsticks upright in rice or shoes on tatami.
- Never use sets of 4 items in a gift scene.
- Never add white chrysanthemums to a festive scene.
- Never use the Rising Sun flag.
