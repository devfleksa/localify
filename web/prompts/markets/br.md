# Brazil (BR)
Copy language: Brazilian Portuguese · Script: Latin with ã õ ç á é í ó ú â ê ô · Currency: R$ 1.299,90 · Date: 14/10/2026 (DD/MM/AAAA) · Numbers: 1.299,90

## Protect
- The "OK" hand sign (thumb and index finger in a circle): it is an obscene gesture in Brazil.
- A price in another currency, a price with a dot as decimal separator, or a date in MM/DD format.
- European Portuguese or Spanish copy presented as Brazilian ("telemóvel", "comboio"; any Spanish).
- Winter or snow themes for June–August as "summer" or Christmas with snow: seasons are reversed (Christmas is in summer).
- The Brazilian flag altered or used as a rag; religious images (Cristo Redentor, saints) used as jokes.

## Language & formats
- **All localized copy in Brazilian Portuguese**, warm, informal and playful ("você", exclamation marks, emojis are fine in captions). Never European Portuguese.
- Portuguese is ~20–30% longer than English: shorten copy to fit the same space.
- Price: "R$ 1.299,90" (space after R$, dot for thousands, comma for decimals). Installments are very common: "ou 10x de R$ 129,99 sem juros".
- Date: 14/10/2026 or "14 de outubro". Time: 24-hour (21h). Units: metric.
- CTAs: "Compre agora", "Garanta o seu", "Confira", "Aproveite", "Frete grátis", "Só hoje", "Novidade".
- Themes that resonate: Carnaval (February/March), Festa Junina (June: bonfires, flags, corn), football and the Seleção (yellow and green), Black Friday (huge), Dia das Mães (May), summer Christmas and New Year on the beach in white clothes.

## Objects & food
Swap for the Brazilian equivalent that plays the same role (same size, position and use). Describe it visually:
- **Barbecue, grill, kebab → churrasco**: brick or steel churrasqueira with long skewers of picanha (cap of beef with fat), sausages (linguiça) and chicken hearts over charcoal, farofa and vinaigrette on the table.
- **Takeaway coffee, mug, tea glass → cafezinho**: small glass or tiny cup of strong black coffee, often with a thermos (garrafa térmica) on the table.
- **Pastry, donut, bagel → pão de queijo or coxinha**: small golden cheese bread balls in a basket; teardrop-shaped fried coxinhas.
- **Burger, sandwich, street food → pastel or açaí bowl**: big rectangular fried pastel at a street market; açaí bowl with banana, granola and condensed milk.
- **Cake, cookies, dessert → brigadeiros**: chocolate truffle balls covered in sprinkles in small paper cups.
- **Breakfast → Brazilian breakfast**: pão francês (crusty rolls) with butter, papaya and mango, cafezinho with milk, pão de queijo.
- **Soft drink → guaraná or fresh juice / coconut water**: green Guaraná-style soda can (unbranded), a fresh coconut with a straw, a tall glass of caldo de cana.
- **Beer or cocktail prop** (only where alcohol is fine for the brand): caipirinha glass with lime and crushed ice.
- **Picnic / beach → Brazilian beach setup**: colourful beach umbrella and folding chairs, a cooler with coconut water, a kanga (sarong) on the sand.
- **Winter holiday decor in December → summer Christmas**: Christmas with sunshine, palm trees, beach; New Year in white with fireworks over the sea.
- **Lucky charm → fitinha do Bonfim** (coloured ribbon wristband from Salvador) or a Festa Junina flag string.

## Places
### Billboard / skyline / big outdoor ad
Note: São Paulo's "Cidade Limpa" law (2007) banned street billboards in São Paulo city. For a billboard scene use Rio de
Janeiro, Belo Horizonte or a highway, or put São Paulo ads on building wraps, bus shelters or metro panels instead.
- **Avenida Paulista, São Paulo (as a street scene)**: wide avenue lined with tall office towers, the MASP museum (red pillars holding a raised concrete box), crowds, bike lane.
- **Rio de Janeiro, Botafogo**: modern buildings along the bay, Sugarloaf Mountain (Pão de Açúcar) rising behind, cable car.
- **Marginal Pinheiros, São Paulo**: Octávio Frias de Oliveira cable-stayed bridge (X-shaped pylon), modern towers along the river.
- **Highway billboard (Rodovia dos Bandeirantes)**: large billboard beside a multi-lane highway, green Brazilian road signs.

### Shopping street / shop window / wall poster
- **Rua Oscar Freire, São Paulo**: Brazil's luxury fashion street: low modern boutiques, wide clean sidewalks, trees. Best match for fashion brands.
- **Vila Madalena, São Paulo**: Beco do Batman alley covered in colourful graffiti; bars and small shops.
- **Ipanema (Rua Visconde de Pirajá), Rio**: shopping street near the beach, palm trees, Portuguese stone sidewalk patterns.
- **Pelourinho, Salvador**: steep cobbled streets, pastel-coloured colonial houses, churches in the distance.
- **Shopping mall (JK Iguatemi, Shopping Leblon)**: bright upscale mall corridors.

### Bus stop / street-level transit
- **São Paulo bus stop on Avenida Paulista**: modern shelter with an ad panel, SPTrans city buses, wide sidewalk.
- **Rio BRT station**: glass tube-shaped station with a ramp, articulated BRT buses.
- **Curitiba tube station**: iconic transparent glass tube bus stops with green or red buses.

### Metro / station
São Paulo and Rio metro stations are busy and practical: grey concrete, coloured line stripes, tiled floors, plain
lighting, ad panels, crowds. Not futuristic.
- **São Paulo Metro, Paulista / Consolação**: line colours (green Line 2, yellow Line 4), long escalators.
- **São Paulo Metro, Sé station**: big open concrete concourse, busy transfer hub.
- **Rio Metro, Cinelândia or General Osório**: orange line signs, platforms with ad frames.

### Roadside / landscape / coast
- **Copacabana promenade, Rio**: wave-pattern black-and-white Portuguese pavement, wide beach, kiosks, hills at the ends.
- **Ipanema beach with Morro Dois Irmãos**: two-peaked mountain at the end of the beach, sunset crowds.
- **Christ the Redeemer (Corcovado)**: statue on the mountain far in the distance only; never as a joke or ad prop.
- **Lençóis Maranhenses**: white sand dunes with blue and green rainwater lagoons.
- **Iguaçu Falls**: wide horseshoe of waterfalls in green jungle, mist, rainbows.
- **Fernando de Noronha**: twin rock spires (Morro Dois Irmãos), turquoise water.
- **Serra do Mar / Rio–Santos road (BR-101)**: coastal road through green Atlantic forest above the sea.
- **Minas Gerais (Ouro Preto)**: colonial town with white houses, coloured window frames, hills.

### Night
Avenida Paulista lit at night, Copacabana with the New Year's fireworks or beach kiosks lit, Lapa Arches (white aqueduct arches) in Rio at night.

## Keep it real
Tropical light, palm trees, black-and-white Portuguese stone sidewalks (Rio), graffiti and pixação on walls (São Paulo),
small padarias (bakeries) and botecos (bars with plastic tables), motorcycles (motoboys), yellow taxis in Rio, dense
apartment towers. Readable signs in correct Brazilian Portuguese; keep them few and short.

## Never
- Never add the "OK" hand sign or change a hand into it.
- Never use Spanish or European Portuguese.
- Never use Christ the Redeemer, saints or the flag as a joke or as part of the product.
- Never add snow to a December scene.
