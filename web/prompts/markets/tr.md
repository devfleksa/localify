# Türkiye (TR)
Copy language: Turkish · Script: Latin with ç ğ ı İ ö ş ü · Currency: ₺1.299,90 or 1.299,90 TL · Date: 14.10.2026 · Numbers: 1.299,90

## Protect
- Alcoholic drinks visible in an ad (beer, wine, cocktails as props): Türkiye bans alcohol advertising (law 6487, 2013).
- Pork, or anything non-halal, on a table or as a prop, especially next to Ramazan, Kurban Bayramı or iftar themes.
- Mosques or other religious buildings used as the backdrop or focus of an ad.
- The Turkish flag, Atatürk or national symbols used playfully, cropped, on the ground or as decoration on a product.
- A price in another currency (USD, EUR) or a date in MM/DD format.

## Language & formats
- **All localized copy in Turkish**, informal "sen" for brand copy aimed at young audiences, "siz" for formal or premium brands. Short and direct.
- Turkish is ~20–30% longer than English: shorten the copy so it fits the same space.
- Price: "1.299,90 TL" or "₺1.299,90" (dot for thousands, comma for decimals). Round prices (e.g. "999 TL") read naturally.
- Date: 14.10.2026 or "14 Ekim". Time: 24-hour (21.00). Units: metric.
- CTAs: "Hemen al", "Keşfet", "Şimdi incele", "Sepete ekle", "Mağazada", "Yeni sezon".
- Always spell Turkish letters correctly (ç, ğ, ı, İ, ö, ş, ü); the dotted capital İ and dotless ı are different letters.

## Objects & food
Swap an object for the Turkish equivalent that plays the same role in the scene (same size, same place, same use).
Describe it visually in the prompt. Swap from → to:
- **Always for TR: outdoor camping, picnic or car-by-nature scenes get a mangal.** Turn the cooking gear (camping stove,
  portable grill, BBQ) into a mangal with şiş kebabs and light smoke: the old gear is removed and the mangal stands exactly
  where it stood, at the same size (on a table: a **small tabletop mangal**, a compact metal charcoal box with a few
  short skewers). Only if the scene has no cooking gear at all, add a small mangal on the ground next to the table.
  This is the post's main object card.
- **Barbecue grill, BBQ, burgers on a grill → mangal**: long rectangular charcoal grill with glowing coals, şiş kebabs and Adana kebabs on flat metal skewers, grilled green peppers and tomatoes, smoke.
- **Coffee cup, mug, takeaway coffee → Turkish tea (çay)**: tulip-shaped clear glass (ince belli bardak) with deep red tea on a small saucer, sugar cubes; optionally a two-tier çaydanlık teapot.
- **Espresso / small coffee → Turkish coffee**: small porcelain cup with thick foam on a copper tray, a glass of water and a piece of lokum (Turkish delight).
- **Donut, bagel, pastry → simit**: ring-shaped bread covered in sesame seeds; or a red simit cart.
- **Burger, hot dog, sandwich → döner or köfte ekmek**: döner wrap (dürüm) or half-bread with grilled köfte, onion and parsley.
- **Pizza → lahmacun or pide**: thin round lahmacun with minced meat and parsley, or boat-shaped pide with melted cheese.
- **Cake, cookies, dessert → baklava or lokum**: diamond-cut baklava with green pistachio on a tray; cubes of lokum dusted with powdered sugar.
- **Breakfast (cereal, pancakes) → Turkish breakfast (kahvaltı)**: many small plates: white cheese, olives, tomatoes, cucumbers, honey and kaymak, eggs in a copper pan (menemen), simit, tea glasses.
- **Soft drink, milkshake → ayran**: frothy white yoghurt drink in a copper cup or a glass.
- **Street snack → roasted chestnuts or corn cart, midye dolma (stuffed mussels with lemon), kumpir (loaded baked potato)**.
- **Decor, lucky charm → nazar boncuğu**: blue glass evil-eye bead; **rug → kilim** with red geometric patterns; **lamp → Ottoman mosaic glass lamp**.
- **Christmas tree / Christmas decor → New Year (Yılbaşı) decor**: in Türkiye the decorated tree is a New Year's Eve tradition; keep it but call it New Year, not Christmas, in any text.

## Places
Pick the place that matches the original scene type, and use a **different place for each slide** of the same post.
Copy the visual cues into the edit prompt: the image model needs to see what the place looks like, a name alone is
not enough. For roadside, nature and outdoor scenes, prefer the famous landscapes outside Istanbul.

### Billboard / skyline / big outdoor ad
- **Levent business district**: glass skyscrapers (Kanyon's curved terraces, İstanbul Sapphire, the Levent towers), wide Büyükdere Avenue below, evening traffic.
- **Zincirlikuyu / Zorlu Center**: modern glass mall façade, Metrobüs line and highway junction, Bosphorus Bridge towers in the distance.
- **Maslak**: tall office towers, wide avenue, rooftop billboards.
- **Bosphorus Bridge (15 Temmuz Şehitler Köprüsü) approach**: red-and-white suspension bridge cables, Bosphorus water, Asian shore hills.
- **Taksim Square**: open square, Atatürk Cultural Center (AKM) red-tile façade, Republic Monument, crowds.

### Shopping street / shop window / wall poster
- **Nişantaşı (Abdi İpekçi Avenue)**: Istanbul's luxury fashion street; tree-lined, Belle Époque apartment façades, designer shop windows, cafés with outdoor tables. Best match for fashion brands.
- **İstiklal Avenue**: busy pedestrian street, 19th-century European-style façades, the red-and-white nostalgic tram on single rails, shop signs in Turkish.
- **Bağdat Avenue (Kadıköy–Suadiye)**: wide upscale shopping avenue on the Asian side, plane trees, flagship stores.
- **Karaköy / Galata streets**: narrow sloping streets, restored stone buildings, street art, cafés, Galata Tower (stone, conical roof) above the rooftops.
- **Kadıköy Moda / Bahariye**: lively neighbourhood streets, colourful shopfronts, the Kadıköy nostalgic tram.
- **Balat**: steep cobbled street with brightly painted Ottoman wooden houses (yellow, red, blue). Very photogenic.

### Bus stop / street-level transit
- **İETT bus stop on a Beşiktaş or Kadıköy main street**: modern shelter with an ad panel, İETT route sign (blue/white), red-and-white or green İETT buses, yellow taxis.
- **Metrobüs stop at Zincirlikuyu**: elevated platform between highway lanes, red articulated Metrobüs buses.
- **Kadıköy ferry pier square (Kadıköy İskelesi)**: historic pier building, ferries, crowds, simit sellers' red carts.

### Metro / station
Istanbul stations are functional and everyday, not futuristic or luxurious. Describe them as they really look: grey speckled
granite floors, off-white or beige wall panels, plain fluorescent ceiling lights, metal benches, simple ad frames, some
wear and scuffs, ordinary commuters. Avoid words like "sleek", "futuristic", "gleaming" or "luxury".
- **M2 Taksim station**: green M2 line colour on signs, plain tiled walls, fluorescent lights, platform with ad frames, signs to "Taksim Meydanı".
- **M2 Şişhane station**: long escalator tunnels, light stone walls, Turkish wayfinding signs ("Çıkış", green M2).
- **Marmaray Üsküdar or Sirkeci station**: deep, large halls, long escalators, red-accent Marmaray signs.
- **Yenikapı transfer station**: wide concourse, signs for several lines (M1, M2, Marmaray), busy commuters.

### Roadside / landscape / coast
- **Bosphorus coastal road (Bebek–Arnavutköy–Rumeli Hisarı)**: two-lane road along the water, wooden waterfront mansions (yalı), Rumeli Fortress stone towers, the Bosphorus Bridge or FSM Bridge.
- **Galata Bridge at dusk**: fishermen with rods along the railing, the Golden Horn, ferries, the old city skyline in the distance (mosques far away only).
- **Üsküdar–Salacak waterfront**: Maiden's Tower (small white tower on a rock in the sea), European shore skyline at sunset.
- **Kuzey Marmara highway / Belgrad Forest road**: modern highway through green forest hills, Yavuz Sultan Selim Bridge (tall A-shaped towers) in the distance.
- **Cappadocia (Göreme)**: fairy chimneys (peri bacaları), cone-shaped rock towers in soft beige/pink stone, dozens of colourful hot-air balloons at sunrise, cave hotels carved into rock.
- **Uludağ (Bursa)**: snowy ski mountain, pine forests, ski slopes and chairlifts, wooden chalet hotels.
- **Erciyes (Kayseri)**: tall snow-capped volcano, modern ski resort, wide open snowy slopes.
- **Mount Ararat (Ağrı Dağı)**: huge lone snow-capped peak above a flat high plain.
- **Pamukkale**: bright white terraced travertine pools with turquoise water, on a hillside.
- **Ölüdeniz (Fethiye)**: turquoise lagoon, white sand spit, pine-covered mountains, colourful paragliders in the sky.
- **Antalya**: Konyaaltı beach with the Taurus mountains rising behind the sea; Kaleiçi old town with narrow streets and the harbour.
- **Bodrum**: white cubic houses with blue shutters on the hills, marina full of wooden gulet boats, Bodrum Castle.
- **Alaçatı (İzmir)**: stone houses with colourful shutters, bougainvillea, old windmills on the hill.
- **İzmir Kordon**: palm-lined seafront promenade, the Konak Clock Tower (ornate Ottoman clock tower).
- **Uzungöl (Trabzon)**: mountain lake in a green valley, wooden houses, mist on steep forested slopes.
- **Safranbolu**: preserved Ottoman town, white and timber-framed houses with red tile roofs.
- **Ankara**: Kızılay's busy avenues; Atakule tower; Anıtkabir's wide stone ceremonial avenue (formal; for respectful, non-playful ads only).
- Road scenes: the Cappadocia valley road, the D400 coastal road along the Mediterranean, mountain roads through Uludağ pines.

### Night
Same places work at night: Levent towers lit up, İstiklal shop lights and the tram, Galata Tower lit in warm light, the Bosphorus Bridge with colour-changing LED lights.

## Keep it real
Every place should look like an everyday photo of the real spot: lived-in, slightly imperfect, normal daylight or street
light, ordinary people in the distance. Not a polished architectural render.
Signage that makes it look local: Turkish street signs (blue with white text: "Cad.", "Sok."), yellow taxis,
red-and-white İETT buses, simit carts, tea glasses on café tables, Turkish flags on buildings (small, not dominant).
Keep background shop signs few and short, or slightly out of focus; any readable sign must be correctly spelled Turkish.

## Never
- Mosques and other religious buildings as the focus behind an ad (keep them out of frame or far in the distance).
- Never swap in alcohol (no rakı, no beer) or pork.
- Never put a food item into a person's mouth or change how a person holds something: keep the hand pose, only the object changes.
- Never change another brand's logo product (e.g. a co-branded cooler): leave it as it is.
